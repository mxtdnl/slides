OUTBREAK · Briefing before the simulation

Run a city
through an outbreak

Twelve weeks, one weekly budget, and every decision binds. You run the response alone.

Welcome. In a few minutes you will each run your own response to an outbreak in a simulated city. This briefing takes about 12 minutes: the goal, the budget, the two kinds of decision, the screen and how to start. It stays high level on purpose: you will learn the detail by playing. Keep laptops closed until the last slide. About 30 seconds.
The situation

A new disease reaches a city of 500,000. You run the response for 12 weeks.

You are the city’s public health director. Each week you choose what to fund and what to restrict.

The city, Meridian City, is fictional, and so is the disease. The simulation models how it spreads week by week, and how your decisions change that. Twelve weeks are simulated; in class each week takes a few minutes. Nothing is undone: once a week has run, it has run. About 1 minute.
Your goal

Protect three things at once

1

Lives

Keep deaths as low as you can, especially among the clinically vulnerable.

40% of your final score

2

Economy

Keep the city working. If the Economy Index ever reaches zero, the run fails.

30% of your final score

3

Satisfaction

Keep the public on side. People who have had enough stop following the rules.

30% of your final score

These three pull against each other, which is the point of the exercise. Shutting everything down saves lives but costs the economy and the public’s patience. Doing nothing keeps the economy going for a while, until the disease takes over. Your final score weights deaths at 40% and the other two at 30% each. If the economy ever hits zero, the run is graded F whatever else you did. About 1.5 minutes.
The budget

You get $10M every week

  • 1

    Use it or lose it. Money left at the end of a week does not carry forward.

  • 2

    No overspending. A week that costs more than the budget cannot be run.

  • 3

    Events cost too. Some responses to an event are paid from that week’s budget.

The weekly budget panel

Screenshots on these slides come from a practice run of OUTBREAK at its default settings.

The budget is $10M a week on the Standard difficulty, and it resets every week. Because unspent money is lost, leaving a lot unspent is usually a missed chance rather than a saving. The panel at the bottom left of the screen shows what you have committed and what is left; the RUN WEEK button will not work if you are over. Some events offer a choice of responses, and the paid ones come out of the same week’s money, so keep a little in reserve. About 1.5 minutes.
Two kinds of decision

Invest in capacity, or restrict how people mix

Decision InvestResource Allocation RestrictPolicy Levers
For exampleHospital beds, staff, testing, vaccines from week 4Lockdown, masks, school closures, travel limits
What it doesTreats the sick and slows the spreadSlows the spread, fast
The catchSome pay off only if bought earlyHurts the economy and satisfaction

Both cost money from the same weekly budget. Public communications and financial support make restrictions easier to bear.

Every decision you make falls into one of these two families. Do not try to learn every slider now: each one has a short description on screen. The key idea is the trade-off. Restrictions work quickly but wear down the economy and public patience, and people comply less as patience runs out. Investments are slower to bite, and some, such as permanent hospital beds and forecasting, only help if bought before you need them. Vaccination unlocks in week 4. About 2 minutes.
The command screen

Everything you need is on one screen

Practice run at the default settings, week 5.

Press r to step through the numbered parts, or press a number. Left is everything you can invest in; right is everything you can restrict. The centre shows where the city stands: cases, deaths, how full the hospitals are, the economy and public satisfaction, and the curve so far. If you fund epidemic modelling, a forecast of next week appears above the curve. Advisors at the bottom right give a hint each week. When the plan is set, press RUN WEEK. About 2 minutes.
One week

Every week follows the same loop

Some weeks open with an event: a strike, a superspreader event, lockdown fatigue. Read it and choose a response; the cheap options usually cost you something else. Then set your plan and press RUN WEEK. The weekly report shows what happened: new cases, deaths, hospital load, the economy and satisfaction. Read it before you press CONTINUE, because it is the best clue to what next week needs. Twelve times round the loop and the run ends. About 1.5 minutes.
The end of the run

After week 12 you get a grade

From the practice run. Your numbers will differ.

Press r to step through the parts. The grade comes from the composite score: deaths count for 40%, the final economy and the final satisfaction for 30% each. The chart puts your run beside three reference strategies the simulation runs for comparison, such as doing nothing. Because everyone plays the same scenario and seed, the class can compare grades fairly afterwards. Write down your grade and composite score before you close the tab. About 1 minute.
