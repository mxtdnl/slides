# OUTBREAK briefing: team play

## Occasion
- Event, host and date: class session that runs the OUTBREAK public health crisis simulation; shown before play; date not supplied (file dated 2026-11 as assumed first delivery)
- Format and length: assumed 12-minute briefing in the room, followed by one shared run of the simulation on the projector
- Room and audience size: in a room, projected; size not supplied
- Accent colour for this deck: `--accent-2` (red)
- Theme: dark, to match the simulation

## Audience
- Who they are: students with no prior knowledge of OUTBREAK, split into six teams: one per interest group (Business, General Public, Vulnerable, Healthcare, Treasury) and a government team
- What they already know: nothing about the simulation, its screens or its scoring
- What they need to leave with: the goal, what each interest group is scored on, how the government is scored (geometric mean of the five group scores), how the weekly budget works, the difference between investing and restricting, how to read the control and debate views, and the weekly loop of argue, decide and report

## Interaction level
- none (presenter reveals only)

## Content
- Frameworks and exact terms: the simulation's own labels, quoted as the app shows them (RUN WEEK, CONTINUE, ASSUME COMMAND, Resource Allocation, Policy Levers, Weekly Budget, Economy Index, Public Satisfaction, Debate, Scores, Class Scorecard)
- How it is run: One shared run on the projector in the Classroom display with 5 lobbies. The government team sits at the projector and operates the controls; the interest-group teams argue for their group each week, using the debate view (D) and the group scoreboard (S). App default settings: scenario Novel Respiratory, difficulty Standard, seed meridian
- Sources approved for citation on slides: the OUTBREAK simulation (`index.html` in the mxtdnl/outbreak repository) and screenshots captured from a practice run of it at the default settings
- Facts or examples that must not be used: anything about real diseases, real cities or real public health responses; detail on every lever and slider (the owner asked for a high-level briefing)

## Voice
- Register, terminology to prefer or avoid: plain English for non-experts, addressed to "you"; short slides, simplicity over completeness; UK English in deck prose
- Anything else the deck must or must not do: the individual and team decks stay largely the same; only the parts about who decides and how scoring works differ
